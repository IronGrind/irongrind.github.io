1 [style=;bold=1] INTRODUCTION
2 [style=Heading2;bold=2] Background of the Study 
3 [style=;bold=0]               The increasing use of information technology has transformed how businesses manage their daily operations, maintain records, and deliver services to customers. In the fitness industry, gym establishments perform various administrative activities, including membership registration, attendance monitoring, payment recording, inventory management, and report preparation. These activities require accurate and organized information to ensure efficient operations and reliable service delivery. As the number of transactions and membership records increases, the use of computerized management systems becomes an important consideration for establishments seeking to improve their administrative processes. 
4 [style=;bold=0]             In this context, IronGrind: A Fitness Gym Membership and Operations Management System also relates to the Sustainable Development Goals through its support for organized gym operations and digital recordkeeping. In relation to SDG 3, Good Health and Well-being, the system supports the administration of gym services by helping members access their membership information and enabling authorized personnel to record attendance. It does not provide medical advice or fitness coaching. For SDG 8, Decent Work and Economic Growth, its organized membership, inventory, sales, and reporting records can support routine administrative work and operational review. In relation to SDG 9, Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure, IronGrind applies web-based technology to the operational needs of a local fitness establishment. The system also relates to SDG 12, Responsible Consumption and Production, by supporting digital membership, attendance, payment, and inventory records that can reduce reliance on paper forms and logbooks. These connections describe the intended contribution of the system rather than measured outcomes of SDG achievement.
5 [style=;bold=0]               Several gym management systems have been developed to support the operational requirements of fitness establishments. For instance, GymMaster reports more than 180,000 active weekly users across over 90 countries (GymMaster, n.d.). Similarly, GYMMANAGER reports operations in 22 countries and more than 600 automated gyms, offering features such as membership management, booking, payment processing, access control, and artificial intelligence-assisted business insights (GYMMANAGER, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). These platforms demonstrate the application of information systems in managing gym operations, particularly in organizing membership records, monitoring attendance, and processing business transactions. 
6 [style=;bold=0]               Despite the availability of digital management solutions, establishments that rely on manual recordkeeping may encounter difficulties in maintaining accurate and accessible information. Paper-based registration forms, attendance logbooks, and separate files can make record retrieval time-consuming and increase the possibility of incomplete, duplicated, or misplaced information. These limitations may affect the efficiency of administrative activities, particularly when staff need to verify membership status, update transactions, monitor attendance, or prepare reports. The use of separate recording methods may also make it difficult to maintain consistent information across different operational activities.
7 [style=;bold=0]               Iron Grind Fitness Gym, located in Purok Mangga, Sulangon, Dapitan City, Zamboanga del Norte, provides fitness services to individuals with varying fitness goals. The establishment offers membership plans, fitness-related guidance, supplements, and gym merchandise. Its daily operations involve managing member registrations, processing membership renewals, recording payments, monitoring attendance, maintaining product inventory, and documenting sales transactions. These interconnected activities require systematic recordkeeping to support the gym owner and staff in managing the establishment effectively.
8 [style=;bold=0]               One of the operational concerns of Iron Grind Fitness Gym involves the management of membership information and transactions. When registration details, membership renewals, payment records, and membership periods are maintained through paper forms or separate files, staff may experience difficulties in organizing and retrieving information. Verifying whether a member has an active membership, confirming renewal payments, and determining membership expiration dates may require checking multiple records. Manual updating of membership information may also result in incomplete records, inconsistent entries, and delays in processing member transactions. These concerns highlight the need for a more organized approach to managing membership information and maintaining accurate membership histories. 
9 [style=;bold=0]               Attendance monitoring presents another operational concern. The use of handwritten attendance logbooks may result in unclear, duplicated, or missing entries, making it difficult to maintain accurate records of members' check-ins and check-outs. Staff may need to review attendance records manually when verifying member visits or preparing attendance reports. Without a centralized attendance monitoring mechanism, retrieving historical attendance information and ensuring consistency between attendance and membership records may become challenging. 
10 [style=;bold=0]                 In addition to membership and attendance management, the gym also handles the sale of supplements and merchandise. Its inventory includes powder, liquid, capsule, and tablet supplements tracked using the applicable weight, volume, piece, or box units, and merchandise such as shirts available in different sizes. Maintaining these products through manual inventory and sales records may create difficulties in monitoring available quantities, recording transactions, and identifying items that require restocking. Since the establishment processes payments in person rather than through an online payment facility, it also requires an organized recording mechanism that supports its existing payment procedures while maintaining accurate sales and inventory information. 
11 [style=;bold=0]                The interconnected nature of these operational concerns emphasizes the need for a centralized information system that can integrate membership management, attendance monitoring, payment recording, inventory management, and reporting. Although commercial gym management platforms provide similar functionalities, Iron Grind Fitness Gym requires a solution designed around its specific operational processes, including membership applications and renewals approved by authorized staff or the administrator, staff-operated attendance recording, inventory tracking using product-appropriate units, and the recording of in-person transactions.
12 [style=;bold=0]                To address these concerns, the proponents developed IronGrind: A Fitness Gym Membership and Operations Management System. The system provides online membership registration, membership application review and approval, membership status monitoring, renewal requests, and member record management. It also incorporates QR code-based attendance monitoring, allowing authorized staff to record member check-ins and check-outs. Members can access their dashboard to view their membership status, remaining membership period, attendance history, and QR code.
13 [style=;bold=0]                 Furthermore, the system supports product inventory management, sales recording, membership transaction monitoring, and report generation. Its inventory features accommodate supplements monitored by weight, volume, piece, or box, as appropriate, and merchandise monitored by item and stock quantity. The system maintains the gym's existing in-person payment process while providing a digital mechanism for recording transactions. Supabase is utilized for authentication and database management, with role-based access controls to support the appropriate use of administrator, staff, and member functions. 
14 [style=;bold=0]                 Through the development of IronGrind, the proponents aim to provide a web-based information system that addresses the operational needs of Iron Grind Fitness Gym. By integrating its essential administrative activities into a centralized platform, the system is intended to minimize difficulties associated with manual recordkeeping, improve the organization and retrieval of information, support accurate membership and attendance monitoring, and facilitate inventory and transaction management. Ultimately, the project seeks to provide a practical and accessible technological solution that supports more efficient gym operations and improved service delivery to its members.
15 [style=Heading2;bold=2] Statement of the Problem 
16 [style=;bold=0]          At present, gym operations involving member registration, membership renewals, payment recording, attendance monitoring, and supplement and merchandise sales may be handled through manual records such as logbooks, paper forms, and separate files. Member information, including contact details, membership plan, payment status, membership dates, and membership status, may be difficult to organize and retrieve when records are not maintained in one centralized system. This can result in delayed transactions, incomplete records, misplaced documents, and errors when updating information.
17 [style=;bold=0]          One major challenge is monitoring membership information, payments, and renewal requests. The administrator needs to review member records, determine membership status, confirm payment records, and monitor expiration dates. Members may submit renewal requests at any time, but each request must be reviewed by authorized staff or the administrator before it takes effect. When records are managed manually, checking these details may take time and affect the speed of customer service. Attendance monitoring through a handwritten logbook can also lead to unclear, duplicate, incomplete, or missing entries.
18 [style=;bold=0]          Supplement and merchandise inventory and sales are also important in the daily operations of Iron Grind Fitness Gym. The gym sells supplements using product-appropriate weight, volume, piece, or box units and merchandise, such as shirts, by item and stock quantity. Manual records may make it difficult for the gym admin to monitor available quantities, record purchases accurately, identify items that need restocking, and review sales information for members and walk-in customers. Since the gym handles payments in person, it also needs an organized way to record completed transactions.
19 [style=;bold=0]            The IronGrind: A Fitness Gym Membership and Operations Management System was developed to address these concerns. The system brings together member and membership management, registration and renewal application review, payment recording, QR code-based attendance monitoring, product inventory, in-person sales transactions, and reports in one web-based platform.
20 [style=;bold=0]            The system organizes member profiles, membership plans, membership dates, and membership status. It allows applicants to submit registration details online for authorized staff or administrator review and allows existing members to submit renewal requests at any time. A renewal takes effect only after authorized staff or administrator approval. If the membership is still active, unused time is carried forward from its current expiry date; if it has expired, the renewed period starts on the approval date.
21 [style=;bold=0]            The system also assigns a QR code to each member. Authorized staff can scan the code to record a member’s check-in or check-out. Members can access their dashboard to view their membership status, membership-period progress, attendance history, and QR code.
22 [style=;bold=0]            Furthermore, the system allows the gym admin to manage supplements tracked using product-appropriate units and merchandise tracked by item and stock quantity. It records completed in-person sales for members and walk-in customers. The system organizes membership, payment, attendance, inventory, and sales information into reports for the gym admin. It does not process payments online.
23 [style=;bold=0]             Therefore, this study aimed to develop a web-based system for Iron Grind Fitness Gym that organizes its membership and operational records. Through the IronGrind system, the gym admin can manage member information, review applications and renewal requests, record attendance and in-person transactions, monitor inventory, and review reports. This can reduce reliance on separate manual records and support more organized day-to-day operations.
24 [style=Heading2;bold=2] Objectives 
25 [style=;bold=0]             The goal of this study was to design and develop IronGrind: A Fitness Gym Membership and Operations Management System for Iron Grind Fitness Gym. The system addresses the need to organize member information, membership applications and renewals, payments, attendance, product inventory, sales transactions, and reports.
26 [style=;bold=0]               Specifically, the proponents aimed to: 
27 [style=ListParagraph;bold=0] To develop a member and membership management feature that creates a centralized database for member profiles, contact information, membership plans, membership start dates, expiration dates, and active or expired membership status. 
28 [style=ListParagraph;bold=0] To develop online registration and renewal request features that allow authorized staff and administrators to review, approve, or reject applications. Members may submit renewal requests at any time, but renewals take effect only after approval by authorized staff or the administrator. An active membership carries forward unused time, while an expired membership restarts on the approval date.
29 [style=ListParagraph;bold=0] To develop a QR code-based attendance monitoring feature that allows authorized staff to record member check-ins and check-outs. 
30 [style=ListParagraph;bold=0] To develop a product inventory and sales feature that manages supplements tracked by weight, volume, piece, or box, as appropriate, and merchandise tracked by item and stock quantity, and records in-person transactions for members and walk-in customers.
31 [style=ListParagraph;bold=0] To develop a reporting feature that organizes system data into separate membership, payment, attendance, inventory, and sales reports, allowing the gym admin to monitor each area of operations individually.
32 [style=Heading2;bold=0] Scope and Delimitation 
33 [style=;bold=0]            The study focused on the development of IronGrind: A Fitness Gym Membership and Operations Management System for Iron Grind Fitness Gym. The system provided a centralized platform for managing member profiles, membership applications and renewals, payments, attendance, product inventory, sales transactions, and reports.
34 [style=;bold=0]             The system stored and organized member profiles and membership information, including membership plans, start dates, expiration dates, and membership status. Applicants could submit registration details online for administrator review. Members could submit renewal requests at any time, but each request required administrator approval. When an active membership was renewed, unused time was carried forward from its current expiry date. When a membership had expired, the renewed period started on the approval date.
35 [style=;bold=0]                The system included a QR code-based attendance monitoring feature. Each member was assigned a QR code that authorized administrators and staff could scan to record check-ins and check-outs using a computer camera or a supported phone camera. Phone scanning required an authorized operator account, with phone pairing managed through the administrator interface. The system stored attendance records for reviewing member visits.
36 [style=;bold=0]               The system also included product inventory and sales features. Supplements were tracked by weight, volume, piece, or box, as appropriate, while merchandise was tracked by item and stock quantity. The gym admin could manage product information and record in-person sales for members and walk-in customers. The system recorded transaction details but did not provide receipts. Payments were handled at the gym; the system did not process payments online.
37 [style=;bold=0]                  The system included a reporting feature that organized membership, payment, attendance, inventory, and sales information into separate reports for the gym admin to review.
38 [style=;bold=0]                   The primary users of the system were the gym administrator, authorized staff, and registered gym members. The administrator managed member records, applications, renewals, attendance, inventory, sales, staff accounts, activity logs, and reports. Authorized staff could record attendance, register walk-in members, process direct membership renewals and plan changes, and record product sales through their assigned functions. Authorized staff and the administrator could approve or reject online registration applications and renewal requests after verifying in-person payment for approvals. Members could view their membership information, membership-period progress, attendance history, and QR code, browse available products, and submit renewal requests. Walk-in customers could have in-person product purchases recorded without using member accounts. These staff membership services include searching by member name or email, selecting the applicable membership plan, and recording the renewal or plan-change transaction. They concern subscription processing and do not permit staff to view or edit member personal profiles. Direct counter processing is separate from online request review. Authorized staff and the administrator can also approve or reject submitted registration applications and renewal requests through Requests approval, without granting staff access to existing member personal profiles.
39 [style=;bold=0]                  The system was limited to Iron Grind Fitness Gym. It did not connect to other gyms, external stores, online payment gateways, banks, or mobile wallet services. It did not provide product reservations, AI-based workout or supplement recommendations, medical advice, or automated fitness coaching. Membership progress referred to the time remaining in a membership period and did not measure workout performance or physical changes.

47 [style=Heading2;bold=3] Definition of terms
48 [style=;bold=1] Administrator. An authorized user responsible for reviewing applications and managing member, attendance, inventory, sales, and report records.
49 [style=;bold=1] Authentication. The verification of a user's sign-in credentials through Supabase Auth before access to IronGrind is granted.
50 [style=;bold=1] Authorization. The determination of which records and operations an authenticated user may access according to the permissions assigned in the system.
51 [style=;bold=1] Authorized Staff. A user assigned a staff account to record attendance, register walk-in members, process direct membership renewals and plan changes, approve or reject online registration and renewal requests, and record in-person product sales within the access allowed by the system.
52 [style=;bold=1] Centralized Database. The shared data store that organizes linked membership, attendance, inventory, and transaction records for IronGrind.
53 [style=;bold=1] Digital Recordkeeping. The electronic storage and retrieval of gym information in place of relying only on paper forms, logbooks, and separate files.
54 [style=;bold=1] Gym Management Application. A software application that supports the administration of gym services, such as membership records, attendance monitoring, inventory, and sales.
55 [style=;bold=1] Gym Member. A registered individual with a member account and recorded membership information.
56 [style=;bold=1] Health Declaration. Health-related information provided by an applicant as part of membership registration; it is recorded for the gym's stated purposes and does not constitute a medical diagnosis.
57 [style=;bold=1] Information Quality. The accuracy, completeness, relevance, and clarity of information presented by a system, considered in this study as a concept for reviewing operational records.
58 [style=;bold=1] Inventory. The recorded quantities of supplements and merchandise available at the gym, monitored using units appropriate to each product.
59 [style=;bold=1] Membership Application. An online submission from a prospective member that awaits administrator review.
60 [style=;bold=1] Membership History. A record of membership events, including renewals, plan changes, and expired membership periods.
61 [style=;bold=1] Membership Progress. A dashboard display showing the time remaining in a member’s recorded membership period.
62 [style=;bold=1] Membership Renewal Request. A request submitted by an existing member to renew their membership. It may be submitted at any time and takes effect only after approval by authorized staff or the administrator. An active membership carries forward unused time, while an expired membership restarts on the approval date.
63 [style=;bold=1] Membership Subscription. A recorded gym membership plan covering a defined period, with a start date and an expiration date.
64 [style=;bold=1] Personal Information. Details recorded about an applicant, member, or operator, such as a name, contact information, and account information.
65 [style=;bold=1] Point of Sale. The stage of an in-person purchase at which the gym records the products, quantities, and transaction amount; IronGrind records the transaction without providing a receipt.
66 [style=;bold=1] PostgreSQL. The relational database technology underlying the Supabase database used to store IronGrind records.
67 [style=;bold=1] QR Code. A two-dimensional machine-readable code assigned to a member and presented for authorized attendance scanning.
68 [style=;bold=1] QR Code-Based Attendance Monitoring. The recording of a member’s check-in or check-out when authorized staff scan the member’s QR code.
69 [style=;bold=1] Reporting Feature. A feature that organizes membership, payment, attendance, inventory, and sales information into separate reports for the gym admin.
70 [style=;bold=1] Role-Based Access Control. The assignment of system permissions according to administrator, staff, and member roles.
71 [style=;bold=1] Row Level Security (RLS). Database rules used in Supabase to control which rows a user may read or modify.
72 [style=;bold=1] Sales Transaction. A recorded in-person purchase containing the customer details, items or membership charges, quantities, and amount, as applicable.
73 [style=;bold=1] Sensitive Personal Information. Health-related declarations and other sensitive details collected during registration that require restricted access and careful handling in the system.
74 [style=;bold=1] Supabase. The backend platform used by IronGrind for authentication, PostgreSQL database storage, and controlled access to records.
75 [style=;bold=1] System Quality. The reliability and usability of system functions, discussed as a concept in reviewing how IronGrind supports gym operations.
76 [style=;bold=1] User Satisfaction. A user's assessment of their experience with the system, discussed as a concept rather than a measured result of this study.
77 [style=;bold=1] Walk-in Customer. An individual who purchases gym products without using a registered member account.
78 [style=;bold=1] Web-Based System. An application accessed through a web browser, allowing authorized users to use IronGrind through supported devices.
