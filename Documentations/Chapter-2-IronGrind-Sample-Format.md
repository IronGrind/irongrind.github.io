# Chapter 2

# REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE

This chapter presents literature and existing systems related to gym membership and operations management. It discusses digital record keeping, membership administration, attendance monitoring, inventory and sales management, reporting, and data protection. The reviewed sources establish the background for IronGrind: A Web-Based Fitness Gym Membership and Operations Management System for Iron Grind Fitness Gym in Purok Mangga, Sulongon, Dapitan City, Zamboanga del Norte. The review focuses on the features and boundaries stated in Chapter 1 and the functions implemented in the current system.

## Related Literature

Commercial gym-management systems show how digital platforms can bring several front-desk and administrative activities together. GymMaster describes features for managing member profiles, memberships, billing, access control, point-of-sale, inventory, and online sign-ups (GymMaster, n.d.). GYMMANAGER likewise presents a system for customer management, sales, access control, facility operations, and analytics (GYMMANAGER, n.d.). These products illustrate the range of functions available in the fitness-management sector. Since these descriptions are published by the vendors themselves, they are treated as examples of system capabilities rather than independent evidence that the products improve operational outcomes. Their relevance to IronGrind is the shared need to organize membership and daily gym operations in an accessible system. Unlike these broad commercial platforms, IronGrind is designed around the specific workflows and scale identified for one local gym.

Centralized information management is important when an organization must retrieve related details from different operational activities. In a gym, member identity may be associated with an application, a selected plan, membership dates, payment records, attendance, and renewal history. If such information is kept in unrelated files, staff may need to search several sources and manually determine which details are current. A database can link records through identifiers and relationships, supporting more consistent retrieval and updates when the information is entered accurately and maintained under clear procedures. IronGrind applies this approach through its Supabase PostgreSQL database, which stores profile, member, membership application, membership history, attendance, product, sale, and sale-item records. These data areas correspond to Chapter 1's objectives to organize member information, monitor membership status, and produce reports from the gym's operational records.

A membership workflow usually involves application intake, review, approval or rejection, an active membership period, and later renewal or expiry. Retaining applications separately from approved membership records allows the organization to keep the decision history instead of treating every submission as an active membership. A record of renewals, expirations, and plan changes also provides information about the membership lifecycle. In IronGrind, applicants submit registration details online and administrators review the applications. Approved details are connected to a member record, and the associated membership transaction is recorded. Members may submit a renewal request only within the five-day period before the existing membership expires; staff review the request, and renewal is not automatic. This workflow reflects Chapter 1's focus on registration, renewal review, and membership history. The system records transactions handled by the gym but does not provide online payment processing.

Attendance information gives gym personnel a record of member visits. A digital attendance process can associate a member with the time of an event and make the resulting history available for authorized review. A QR code can be used as an identifier during this process, but scanning alone does not define who is permitted to record attendance or how duplicate and incomplete entries should be handled. IronGrind uses staff-operated QR scanning: staff scan a member's code to record check-in and check-out, while members can view their own attendance history through the member portal. This matches the Chapter 1 requirement for staff scanning and does not represent member self-check-in or automated gate access. Attendance records are stored separately from membership records so that visits remain a distinct operational history.

Product management and point-of-sale activities also require consistent records. A gym may sell merchandise counted by item and supplements sold by a measured quantity, so the product unit and stock rules need to correspond to the product category. A transaction record can preserve its receipt information, customer, items, quantities, and totals, supporting later review and reporting. IronGrind distinguishes supplements from merchandise: supplements are sold by gram, while merchandise is tracked by item and stock quantity. Staff record product and membership sales at the gym, including walk-in transactions where applicable. The system stores sales and their line items, and merchandise quantities are updated through the sales workflow. Online product checkout and payment processing are outside the project's stated scope.

Operational reports organize stored data so that administrators can review information such as membership status, applications, renewals, attendance, inventory, and sales. The usefulness of a report depends on the completeness and consistency of the source records and on clear definitions for fields such as membership status, transaction date, and product unit. IronGrind's reporting functions are based on the records maintained by its operational modules. Chapter 1 identifies reports for membership, member status, archives, payments, attendance, renewals, inventory, and sales. These are intended to help the gym's owner and authorized staff retrieve the information recorded by the system. The presence of reports does not, by itself, demonstrate improvements in accuracy, processing time, or business performance; those outcomes require a separate evaluation and evidence.

The DeLone and McLean Information Systems Success Model provides a framework for examining information systems through dimensions such as system quality, information quality, service quality, use, user satisfaction, and net benefits (DeLone & McLean, 2003). The model is relevant to IronGrind because a system's value depends not only on the existence of its pages and database, but also on whether its functions work for intended users and provide useful, dependable information. For this study, system quality may be examined through the operation of the browser workflows, authentication, and database-backed modules. Information quality concerns whether member, attendance, inventory, and sales records are complete and consistent enough for their intended uses. Use, user satisfaction, and net benefits are evaluation considerations, not established findings. They should be reported only if they are assessed using the methods described in Chapter 3.

Security and privacy are important because the system stores account, contact, emergency-contact, and health-related information. PostgreSQL row-level security policies can restrict the records that a database role may read or modify, and Supabase documents the use of Row Level Security together with Supabase Auth to apply access rules to database requests (PostgreSQL Global Development Group, n.d.; Supabase, n.d.). IronGrind uses Supabase Auth for identity and session management, profile roles to distinguish administrators and members, and database authorization for permitted operations. Privileged staff-created account operations are routed through a server-side Edge Function that checks the administrator role. The effectiveness of these protections depends on the deployed policies and configuration and should be verified through role-based tests.

The Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 establishes principles for processing personal information, including transparency, legitimate purpose, and proportionality, and identifies health information as sensitive personal information (Republic Act No. 10173, 2012, secs. 3, 11, 13). These principles are relevant because IronGrind's application records may contain health declarations and other personal details. The gym should explain the purposes for collecting information, limit collection and access to legitimate operational needs, protect the records, and establish appropriate retention and handling procedures. Describing these obligations does not certify legal compliance; compliance depends on the organization's actual notices, safeguards, retention practices, and governance.

## Synthesis

The reviewed literature and systems show that gym administration connects member information, application and renewal decisions, attendance, inventory, sales, and operational reports. Commercial gym platforms demonstrate that these are recognized areas of fitness-facility management, while information-systems literature highlights the importance of system quality, useful information, user experience, and assessable outcomes. Technical documentation further establishes that authenticated access and database-level row policies can support role-based data protection. Philippine privacy law is also relevant because the system processes personal and health-related information.

These sources support the development of IronGrind as a web-based system that organizes the operational functions specified for Iron Grind Fitness Gym. The system connects profiles, members, applications, membership history, attendance, products, sales, and sale items in a Supabase database. It provides online registration and renewal submissions for staff review, staff-scanned QR attendance, member access to selected account and attendance information, product management with separate units for supplements and merchandise, in-person sales records, and administrative reports. The relationship between these features and the objectives in Chapter 1 keeps the proposed system focused on the needs identified in the study.

The reviewed commercial platforms offer broader capabilities than IronGrind, such as payment integrations, access-control hardware, or dedicated mobile applications. Those functions are not assumed to be part of this project. IronGrind is limited to the workflows described in Chapter 1: it does not provide an online payment gateway, online product checkout, member-operated attendance entry, automated fitness coaching, or a native mobile application. The need addressed by the study is a system tailored to the gym's stated membership and operations tasks, rather than a reproduction of a comprehensive commercial platform. Any claims that the system improves efficiency, accuracy, or user satisfaction must be supported by the evaluation conducted for the study.
