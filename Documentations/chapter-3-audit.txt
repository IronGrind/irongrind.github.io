1 [style=;bold=2] RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
2 [style=;bold=0] This chapter discusses the methodology used to develop IronGrind: A Web-Based Fitness Gym Membership and Operations Management System for Iron Grind Fitness Gym in Purok Mangga, Sulangon, Dapitan City, Zamboanga del Norte. It describes the development process, system requirements, design, data organization, and evaluation procedures. The system is intended to organize membership applications and records, membership periods and renewals, staff-recorded attendance, products, sales, and reports. Its development is organized using Agile, with work planned and delivered incrementally. Proposed evaluation activities are distinguished from results that have not yet been collected.
3 [style=Heading2;bold=2] System Development Methodology
4 [style=;bold=0] 
5 [style=;bold=0] Figure 1. Agile
6 [style=;bold=0] Agile is an iterative system-development approach in which requirements guide the work, features are built in manageable increments, and the results of testing and review guide subsequent work. As shown in Figure 1, the process begins by planning IronGrind's workflows and priorities and designing the related pages, forms, data relationships, and access permissions. The team then develops a selected group of features and tests them against their requirements. Deployment in the cycle means preparing and checking an increment in its intended environment; review considers the test findings and any feedback actually collected. Those findings return to planning and help determine what to refine or build next. This cycle applies to the system's membership applications and renewals, attendance, inventory, sales, and reports. The final Launch stage represents release of the approved system for operational use after the required increments have been verified. Figure 1 describes this project's workflow; it does not claim use of a specific Agile framework or a fixed iteration length. Evaluation results are reported only after the planned evaluation activities have been completed.
7 [style=Heading2;bold=2] Requirements Planning
8 [style=;bold=0] Requirements planning identifies separate workflows for applicants, members, administrators, and authorized staff. Requirements cover online applications subject to authorized staff or administrator review, direct counter registration and membership changes, QR attendance, product inventory, transaction recording, reporting, staff accounts, phone pairing, and activity logs. Acceptance criteria include role permissions, required inputs, stock and membership updates, and rejection of unauthorized operations. These criteria guide each development increment.
9 [style=Heading2;bold=2] User Design
10 [style=;bold=0] Using the prioritized requirements, the team designs or refines the corresponding user workflows, page layouts, forms, reports, data relationships, and role permissions. For example, the design distinguishes pending applications from approved member records, keeps attendance as a separate operational record, and stores sales with their itemized lines. The architecture, use-case, and data-flow figures in this chapter summarize how these parts fit together. These designs are then represented in prototypes so that the intended workflows can be reviewed before or during construction.
11 [style=Heading2;bold=2] Prototype
12 [style=;bold=0] The prototype represents the designed workflows through browser-based pages. Applicants submit registration details, while members view their membership, attendance, QR code, profile, catalog, and renewal requests. Administrators view member profiles without editing personal details, review online requests, perform membership operations, and handle inventory, sales, staff access, phone pairing, activity logs, and reports. Staff use a separate portal for attendance, walk-in registration, direct membership renewals and active-member plan changes, product sales, their own product-sale records, and online registration and renewal request approval. The system records in-person transactions without providing receipts to customers. Supabase Auth, database policies, functions, and authorized Edge Functions support the implemented operations.
13 [style=Heading2;bold=2] Construction
14 [style=;bold=0] The team constructs the selected increment using the HTML, CSS, and JavaScript client and Supabase authentication and PostgreSQL services. Database policies and functions support authorization and operational transactions. The completed increment is tested against the acceptance criteria established during planning. If a test identifies an issue, the team corrects it and checks it again; unresolved or newly discovered needs are recorded for prioritization. Once the increment passes the required checks, it can be prepared in the intended environment and reviewed. Test findings and any feedback actually collected inform the next planning cycle, connecting construction to continued refinement.
15 [style=Heading2;bold=2] Implementation
16 [style=;bold=0] Implementation prepares the verified increments for operational use. The team configures the website and Supabase project for the intended environment, applies the approved access policies, and prepares administrator access. Deployment refers to this environment preparation and release check; Launch is the point at which the approved system is made available for the gym's intended workflows. Staff orientation supports the transition to use. These activities follow testing and review, and their outcomes are reported only after they have actually been completed.

59 [style=ListParagraph;bold=2] 1.0 Authenticate and Authorize
60 [style=;bold=0] Supabase Auth verifies credentials and establishes the user session. The system reads the profile role and active-account state to route the account to the administrator, staff, or member interface. Authorization is enforced through database policies, role-checked functions, and Edge Functions, rather than relying on page visibility alone.
61 [style=;bold=0] 2.0 Review Online Applications. Applicants submit registration details and health declarations to membership_applications. Only administrators review online requests and approve or reject them. Approval creates or links the member record, updates the application status, and records the applicable membership payment through sales and sale_items. Direct walk-in registration follows process 3.0 instead of granting staff online-review permission.
62 [style=;bold=0] 3.0 User Management. Administrators can view member profiles but cannot edit their personal information. They perform authorized membership operations and create or activate/deactivate staff accounts through authorized services. Members can retrieve and update only their own permitted personal details. Staff have a separate labeled counter flow: walk-in registration, limited member lookup, direct renewal, and active-member plan change. Staff cannot view or edit existing member profiles. Account rows created by the registration service describe backend storage, not profile-editing permission granted to staff. Membership changes record applicable member, history, sales, and sale-item updates.
63 [style=;bold=0] 4.0 Review Online Renewals. Members submit renewal requests to membership_applications. Administrators approve or reject these requests. An approved renewal preserves unused time by extending an active membership from its current expiry date; an expired membership restarts on approval. Approval updates the member, history, application status, and membership transaction records. Direct counter renewals by an administrator or staff operator belong to process 3.0 and do not grant staff permission to review online requests.
64 [style=;bold=0] 5.0 Record and View Attendance. An authorized administrator or staff operator scans a member QR code with a supported computer or phone camera. The database validates the operator and member, checks for an open visit, and records check-in or check-out in attendance. recorded_by and checked_out_by identify the operators for each event. Private attendance_scan_requests records support duplicate-request protection. Paired phones additionally use administrator-managed pairing sessions and policy; pairing does not replace operator authentication. Members can read their own attendance history. Authorized staff and administrators read attendance within their permitted scope. Administrator-only phone-pairing creation, settings and removal belong to this attendance workflow. Staff and members do not receive those administration controls.
65 [style=;bold=0] 6.0 Manage Inventory and Catalog. Administrators maintain product details, prices, stock, product forms, and active or archived state. Powder supplements support weight units; drinkable supplements support volume units; capsules and tablets use pieces or boxes; other supplements and merchandise use items. Sale processing converts compatible quantities and deducts stock using the configured inventory units. Members browse active products, and staff obtain the permitted catalog needed for sales; these read operations do not grant inventory-maintenance permission.
66 [style=;bold=3] 7.0 Sales
67 [style=;bold=0] Administrators and staff record in-person product purchases for members or walk-in customers. The sale operation validates quantities and product status, writes sales and sale_items, and deducts product stock. Membership charges are recorded through the membership operations in processes 2.0 to 4.0. The database retains the existing receipt_number field as an internal transaction identifier. The documented operating process does not provide customers with receipts.
68 [style=;bold=2] 8.0 Generate Reports
69 [style=;bold=0] Administrators select a report type and period to retrieve the relevant operational records. Supported outputs include preview, print layout, individual PDFs, and combined ZIP export. This reporting process does not manage staff access, phone pairings, or activity-log filters.
70 [style=;bold=0] 9.0 Activity Logs. Administrators review database-recorded actions using date, account, action, and area filters and pagination. Logs include recorded staff-account changes and phone-pairing events. The interface reads activity_logs; it does not create staff accounts, alter pairing settings, or modify the recorded events. Those operations occur in User Management or QR Attendance and produce their own log entries.

107 [style=;bold=0] Figure 5a-c. Physical entity relationships
108 [style=;bold=0] The physical ERD in Figure 5a-c explains the relationships declared in the checked-in database migration baseline. It complements the DFD: the DFD shows how information is processed, whereas the ERD shows how stored records refer to one another. Each relationship row names the parent and child entity, identifies the referenced primary key and child foreign key, and states its cardinality and deletion rule. The panels repeat entity boxes where necessary to keep the relationships readable; repeated names denote the same physical table and should not be counted as additional tables.
109 [style=;bold=0] Cardinality describes the number of records permitted on either side of a relationship. A required foreign key means that a child record must reference one parent; a nullable foreign key permits the child to exist without that reference. A parent may generally be associated with multiple child records, although a primary key or unique index can restrict a relationship to at most one child. For example, a member may have many attendance records and membership-history events, while the linked Auth-account index permits at most one member record for a particular non-null Auth identifier. An optional sale member reference and an optional sale-line product reference must not be treated as mandatory links.
110 [style=;bold=0] The core entities preserve different kinds of operational information. Membership applications retain submitted registration or renewal requests and their review status. Members retains the current member and membership values, while membership_history preserves relevant values before and after lifecycle events. Attendance stores visits and the separate operators for check-in and check-out. Sales stores transaction headers, and sale_items stores the individual product or membership charges. Product names and prices captured in sale lines support historical records even when the current catalog changes. The retained receipt_number field is documented as an internal transaction identifier rather than evidence that a customer receipt is issued.
111 [style=;bold=0] The physical model also includes supporting account, phone-pairing, logging, and attendance-request storage. The dictionary distinguishes twelve public-schema tables from two private supporting tables; managed Auth internals are outside the application-owned table count. The retained mobile_attendance_scan_events table appears because it exists in the baseline, not because a corresponding review interface is available. Deletion rules follow the actual constraints: CASCADE removes dependent rows, SET NULL clears an optional reference, and RESTRICT or the default NO ACTION behavior can prevent a change that would break a relationship. Finally, a foreign-key relationship does not grant an actor permission to view or edit data. Administrator read-only profile viewing, member-only personal editing, and the staff profile restriction remain application authorization rules.
112 [style=;bold=0] 1. auth.users Entity This entity serves as the primary authentication table for the system. It stores the unique identifier (id) and email address of every user. This entity is crucial for system security, ensuring that only individuals with verified credentials can log in and access the system.
113 [style=;bold=0] 2. profiles Entity. Each profile shares its primary key with auth.users and records the administrator, staff, or member role, account status, active flag, name, username, email, phone number, gender, and timestamps.
114 [style=;bold=0] 3. membership_applications Entity This table stores data from new applicants and renewal requests awaiting review by authorized staff or the administrator. It tracks the application type, selected plan, current status, and submission time. It links to the user applying (auth_user_id) and the staff member or administrator who reviewed it (reviewed_by).
115 [style=;bold=0] 4. members Entity The members table stores each member’s personal information, current membership plan, membership dates, status, and registration source. It links to the member’s Supabase Auth account and may reference a registration application. The application reference can be empty for members created directly by an administrator.
116 [style=;bold=0] 5. attendance Entity. Each visit references a member and stores its date and check-in and optional check-out timestamps. recorded_by and checked_out_by independently reference the Auth accounts responsible for the two attendance events.
117 [style=;bold=0] 6. products Entity. Products store category, price, stock, stock and sale units, supplement form and type, merchandise type, size, active or archived state, and image reference. Stock is numeric and supports product-appropriate weight, volume, piece, box, or item units.
118 [style=;bold=0] 7. sales Entity. Each row stores a transaction total, customer details, optional member reference, status, sold_at timestamp, and processing Auth account. The retained receipt_number column is an internal transaction reference; its technical name does not mean that a receipt is provided to the customer.
119 [style=;bold=0] 8. sale_items Entity. Each line belongs to one sale and optionally references a product. It preserves the item or membership label, category, quantity, unit, price, and line total. Membership charges have no product reference, and historical item labels remain available if a product reference becomes NULL.
120 [style=;bold=0] 9. membership_history Entity. History records reference a member and optionally the responsible Auth account. They retain renewal, plan-change, or expiry events and the membership values before and after the event.
121 [style=;bold=0] Auth Users to Profiles and Members. An Auth account can have at most one profile and at most one linked member record. Each profile requires an Auth account; a member record can exist without an Auth link. Administrator, staff, and pending-applicant accounts need not have a linked member record.
122 [style=;bold=0] Members to Membership Applications. This is a one-to-many relationship: a member can have multiple membership applications over time, including renewal requests. Each application may reference one member. A pending registration application can exist without a linked member record until it is processed.
123 [style=;bold=0] Members to Attendance. This is a one-to-many relationship because a member can have multiple attendance logs (check-ins) over their membership period, but each attendance record is tied to only one member.
124 [style=;bold=0] Members to Sales. This is a one-to-many relationship because a member can make multiple product purchases (sales), but each sale is tied to only one member (if it is not a walk-in).
125 [style=;bold=0] Members to Membership History. This is a one-to-many relationship because a member can have multiple history records (renewals, plan changes) over time, but each history record is linked to only one member.
126 [style=;bold=0] Sales to Sale_Items. This is a one-to-many relationship because a single sales transaction can contain multiple items (sale_items), but each sale_item belongs to only one sale.
127 [style=;bold=0] Products to Sale_Items. A product can appear in multiple sale items. Each sale item may reference one product, while membership charges have no product reference. The item name and price are retained as transaction snapshots
128 [style=;bold=0] Supporting Relationships. Online application reviewed_by references profiles, while attendance and transaction operator fields reference auth.users. Phone sessions reference their creator, scan events reference their session, and pairing policy optionally references its updating Auth user. Private attendance_scan_requests references an operator and member. activity_logs.actor_id is a stored actor identifier without a declared foreign key; it is not drawn as an enforced relationship.
129 [style=;bold=2] Overall Description
130 [style=;bold=0] The ERD includes core membership and transaction relationships and supporting identity and phone-attendance relationships. Nullable references permit walk-in sales without a member, membership charges without a product, and membership records without an Auth account. Referential actions follow the baseline: attendance.member_id cascades on member deletion, while membership_history.member_id restricts deletion. The dictionary below lists actual column types, NULLability, and declared keys from the same baseline rather than assuming that similarly named fields have identical types.

156 [style=Heading2;bold=2] Forms
157 [style=;bold=0] The system uses registration, profile, renewal, member, inventory, sales, staff-account, and phone-pairing forms. Members submit online registration or renewal information and maintain their permitted profile details. Administrators review online requests, maintain products and stock, and manage staff and pairing access. Authorized staff use counter forms for walk-in registration, direct renewal, active-member plan change, and product purchases, and use Requests approval for online registration and renewal decisions. Attendance uses camera scanning under an authorized operator account. Completed payments and purchases are recorded as transactions; receipts are not provided to customers. Required fields, validation, and server-side role checks support consistent data entry.
