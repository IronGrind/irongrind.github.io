P0165: INTRODUCTION
P0166: Background of the Study
P0167: The increasing use of information technology has transformed how businesses manage their daily operations, maintain records, and deliver services to customers. In the fitness industry, gym establishments perform various administrative activities, including membership registration, attendance monitoring, payment recording, inventory management, and report preparation. These activities require accurate and organized information to ensure efficient operations and reliable service delivery. As the number of transactions and membership records increases, the use of computerized management systems becomes an important consideration for establishments seeking to improve their administrative processes.
P0168: In this context, IronGrind: A Fitness Gym Membership and Operations Management System also relates to the Sustainable Development Goals through its support for organized gym operations and digital recordkeeping. In relation to SDG 3, Good Health and Well-being, the system supports the administration of gym services by helping members access their membership information and enabling authorized personnel to record attendance. It does not provide medical advice or fitness coaching. For SDG 8, Decent Work and Economic Growth, its organized membership, inventory, sales, and reporting records can support routine administrative work and operational review. In relation to SDG 9, Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure, IronGrind applies web-based technology to the operational needs of a local fitness establishment. The system also relates to SDG 12, Responsible Consumption and Production, by supporting digital membership, attendance, payment, and inventory records that can reduce reliance on paper forms and logbooks. These connections describe the intended contribution of the system rather than measured outcomes of SDG achievement.
P0169: Several gym management systems have been developed to support the operational requirements of fitness establishments. For instance, GymMaster describes membership management, billing, point-of-sale, online sign-ups, and access-control features (GymMaster, n.d.). Similarly, GYMMANAGER describes sales, front-desk operations, scheduling, access control, and reporting within a centralized platform (GYMMANAGER, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). These platforms demonstrate the application of information systems in managing gym operations, particularly in organizing membership records, monitoring attendance, and recording business transactions.
P0170: Despite the availability of digital management solutions, establishments that rely on manual recordkeeping may encounter difficulties in maintaining accurate and accessible information. Paper-based registration forms, attendance logbooks, and separate files can make record retrieval time-consuming and increase the possibility of incomplete, duplicated, or misplaced information. These limitations may affect the efficiency of administrative activities, particularly when staff need to verify membership status, update transactions, monitor attendance, or prepare reports. The use of separate recording methods may also make it difficult to maintain consistent information across different operational activities.
P0171: Iron Grind Fitness Gym, located in Purok Mangga, Sulangon, Dapitan City, Zamboanga del Norte, provides fitness services to individuals with varying fitness goals. The establishment offers membership plans, fitness-related guidance, supplements, and gym merchandise. Its daily operations involve managing member registrations, processing membership renewals, recording payments, monitoring attendance, maintaining product inventory, and documenting sales transactions. These interconnected activities require systematic recordkeeping to support the gym owner and staff in managing the establishment effectively.
P0172: One of the operational concerns of Iron Grind Fitness Gym involves the management of membership information and transactions. When registration details, membership renewals, payment records, and membership periods are maintained through paper forms or separate files, staff may experience difficulties in organizing and retrieving information. Verifying whether a member has an active membership, confirming renewal payments, and determining membership expiration dates may require checking multiple records. Manual updating of membership information may also result in incomplete records, inconsistent entries, and delays in processing member transactions. These concerns highlight the need for a more organized approach to managing membership information and maintaining accurate membership histories.
P0173: Attendance monitoring presents another operational concern. The use of handwritten attendance logbooks may result in unclear, duplicated, or missing entries, making it difficult to maintain accurate records of members' check-ins and check-outs. Staff may need to review attendance records manually when verifying member visits or preparing attendance reports. Without a centralized attendance monitoring mechanism, retrieving historical attendance information and ensuring consistency between attendance and membership records may become challenging.
P0174: In addition to membership and attendance management, the gym also handles the sale of supplements and merchandise. Its inventory includes powder, liquid, capsule, and tablet supplements tracked using the applicable weight, volume, piece, or box units, and merchandise such as shirts available in different sizes. Maintaining these products through manual inventory and sales records may create difficulties in monitoring available quantities, recording transactions, and identifying items that require restocking. Since the establishment processes payments in person rather than through an online payment facility, it also requires an organized recording mechanism that supports its existing payment procedures while maintaining accurate sales and inventory information.
P0175: The interconnected nature of these operational concerns emphasizes the need for a centralized information system that can integrate membership management, attendance monitoring, payment recording, inventory management, and reporting. Although commercial gym management platforms provide similar functionalities, Iron Grind Fitness Gym requires a solution designed around its specific operational processes, including membership applications and renewals approved by authorized staff or the administrator, staff-operated attendance recording, inventory tracking using product-appropriate units, and the recording of in-person transactions.
P0176: To address these concerns, the proponents developed IronGrind: A Fitness Gym Membership and Operations Management System. The system provides online membership registration, membership application review and approval, membership status monitoring, renewal requests, and member record management. It also incorporates QR code-based attendance monitoring, allowing authorized staff to record member check-ins and check-outs. Members can access their dashboard to view their membership status, remaining membership period, attendance history, and QR code.
P0177: Furthermore, the system supports product inventory management, sales recording, membership transaction monitoring, and report generation. Its inventory features accommodate supplements monitored by weight, volume, piece, or box, as appropriate, and merchandise monitored by item and stock quantity. The system maintains the gym's existing in-person payment process while providing a digital mechanism for recording transactions. Supabase is utilized for authentication and database management, with role-based access controls to support the appropriate use of administrator, staff, and member functions.
P0178: Through the development of IronGrind, the proponents aim to provide a web-based information system that addresses the operational needs of Iron Grind Fitness Gym. By integrating its essential administrative activities into a centralized platform, the system is intended to minimize difficulties associated with manual recordkeeping, improve the organization and retrieval of information, support accurate membership and attendance monitoring, and facilitate inventory and transaction management. Ultimately, the project seeks to provide a practical and accessible technological solution that supports more efficient gym operations and improved service delivery to its members.
P0179: Statement of the Problem
P0180: At present, gym operations involving member registration, membership renewals, payment recording, attendance monitoring, and supplement and merchandise sales may be handled through manual records such as logbooks, paper forms, and separate files. Member information, including contact details, membership plan, payment status, membership dates, and membership status, may be difficult to organize and retrieve when records are not maintained in one centralized system. This can result in delayed transactions, incomplete records, misplaced documents, and errors when updating information.
P0181: One major challenge is monitoring membership information, payments, and renewal requests. The administrator needs to review member records, determine membership status, confirm payment records, and monitor expiration dates. Members may submit renewal requests at any time, but each request must be reviewed by authorized staff or the administrator before it takes effect. When records are managed manually, checking these details may take time and affect the speed of customer service. Attendance monitoring through a handwritten logbook can also lead to unclear, duplicate, incomplete, or missing entries.
P0182: Supplement and merchandise inventory and sales are also important in the daily operations of Iron Grind Fitness Gym. The gym sells supplements using product-appropriate weight, volume, piece, or box units and merchandise, such as shirts, by item and stock quantity. Manual records may make it difficult for the gym admin to monitor available quantities, record purchases accurately, identify items that need restocking, and review sales information for members and walk-in customers. Since the gym handles payments in person, it also needs an organized way to record completed transactions.
P0183: The IronGrind: A Fitness Gym Membership and Operations Management System was developed to address these concerns. The system brings together member and membership management, registration and renewal application review, payment recording, QR code-based attendance monitoring, product inventory, in-person sales transactions, and reports in one web-based platform.
P0184: The system organizes member profiles, membership plans, membership dates, and membership status. It allows applicants to submit registration details online for authorized staff or administrator review and allows existing members to submit renewal requests at any time. A renewal takes effect only after authorized staff or administrator approval. If the membership is still active, unused time is carried forward from its current expiry date; if it has expired, the renewed period starts on the approval date.
P0185: The system also assigns a QR code to each member. Authorized staff can scan the code to record a member’s check-in or check-out. Members can access their dashboard to view their membership status, membership-period progress, attendance history, and QR code.
P0186: Furthermore, the system allows the gym admin to manage supplements tracked using product-appropriate units and merchandise tracked by item and stock quantity. It records completed in-person sales for members and walk-in customers. The system organizes membership, payment, attendance, inventory, and sales information into reports for the gym admin. It does not process payments online.
P0187: Therefore, this study aimed to develop a web-based system for Iron Grind Fitness Gym that organizes its membership and operational records. Through the IronGrind system, the gym admin can manage member information, review applications and renewal requests, record attendance and in-person transactions, monitor inventory, and review reports. This can reduce reliance on separate manual records and support more organized day-to-day operations.

P0196: Scope and Delimitation
P0197: The study focused on the development of IronGrind: A Fitness Gym Membership and Operations Management System for Iron Grind Fitness Gym. The system provided a centralized platform for managing member profiles, membership applications and renewals, payments, attendance, product inventory, sales transactions, and reports.
P0198: The system stored member profiles and membership information, including plans, membership dates, and status. Applicants could submit online registration details for authorized staff or administrator review. Members could submit online renewal requests at any time, with approval required before a request took effect. Approval of an online renewal extended an active subscription from its current expiry date; an expired subscription restarted on the approval date. Direct counter renewal and active-member plan changes were separate operator transactions using the permitted plan, price, and membership dates.
P0199: The system included QR code-based attendance monitoring. Authorized administrators and staff could record member check-ins and check-outs using a supported computer or phone camera. Staff could also search by member name or Gmail, select the matching record, and submit attendance using its unique member identifier. Search alone did not record attendance or provide general member-profile access. A phone required an authorized operator sign-in and an administrator-managed pairing. Each phone browser installation used its own pairing QR and record. A setup QR expired after ten minutes; successful phone pairing remained valid for thirty days unless removed earlier. Paired phones and unexpired pending QRs shared the configured slot limit, initially set to two. A pending QR reserved one slot until paired, ended, or expired. The attendance scanner could be opened through a separate installation link and installed as a Progressive Web App; reinstalling in the same browser could reuse an available, active saved pairing. Cleared browser data, removed pairings, or expired connections could require administrator-assisted pairing again. Attendance without a check-out from a previous Philippine calendar date was displayed as Incomplete; the system did not invent a missing check-out timestamp.
P0200: The system also included product inventory and sales features. Supplements were tracked by weight, volume, piece, or box, as appropriate, while merchandise was tracked by item and stock quantity. The gym admin could manage product information and record in-person sales for members and walk-in customers. The system recorded transaction details but did not provide receipts. Payments were handled at the gym; the system did not process payments online.
P0201: Low-stock alerts used product-form and selling-unit rules, with conversion from the configured stock unit, including pounds, to the applicable base unit. Initial thresholds were 1,000 g for protein powder; 250 g for creatine, pre-workout, amino-acid, and vitamin powders; 500 g for other powders; 1,000 ml for liquids; 30 individually sold capsules or tablets; four boxes; ten other supplement items; and four merchandise items per product or size. These were configurable-in-code starting alert levels, not calculated forecasts or proven optimal reorder points. Actual reorder levels required sales-demand and supplier-lead-time information.
P0202: The system included a reporting feature that organized membership, payment, attendance, inventory, and sales information into separate reports for the gym admin to review.
P0203: The primary users of the system were the gym administrator, authorized staff, and registered gym members. The administrator could view member personal profiles without editing their personal details and manage membership operations, attendance, inventory, sales, staff accounts, phone pairings, activity logs, and reports. Authorized staff could record attendance, register walk-in members, process direct renewals and active-member plan changes using limited member lookup, record product sales, and review their own product-sale records. Both staff and administrators could approve or reject online registration and renewal requests, confirming in-person payment before approval. Staff could not view or edit existing member personal profiles. Members could view and update their own permitted personal details, access their membership status, attendance history and QR code, browse active products, and request renewal. Walk-in product purchases could be recorded without a member account.
P0204: Account creation included validation of required information, matching password confirmation, password-strength requirements, and duplicate email addresses or, where supported, usernames. Staff account registration collected first name, optional middle initial, and last name separately and combined them into the existing full-name record. Administrator and staff walk-in member forms used the same separate-name arrangement without introducing separate database name columns. Names containing numeric characters were rejected. Member password creation and updates used the implemented eight-character minimum; staff-account creation used a twelve-character minimum, with the applicable uppercase, lowercase, digit, and symbol requirements. Show/hide controls improved password entry without storing readable passwords. Administrator and staff accounts had to be active to use their protected functions. Members remained restricted to their own permitted personal information.
P0205: Administrator trusted-browser security. The current revision authorizes one trusted browser per administrator account for thirty days. First registration requires a fresh authenticator code. Returning to the same browser requires the password and automatic browser-key proof. A replacement browser requests approval from an eligible active trusted session and then requires fresh authenticator verification; successful replacement revokes the previous trust and dashboard/scanner scopes. If no eligible approving session is recently active, the implemented fallback uses fresh authenticator verification. Normal logout retains trust; explicit forget-device logout revokes it. Staff and member login remain role-specific. In-website notifications require an active connection; SMS and operating-system push are outside this implementation. Three private security tables support approval, trusted public keys and session challenges. Authenticator loss requires verified project-owner recovery. Migration and function deployment are evidenced by user-supplied CLI output; live authenticator and two-browser acceptance tests remain to be completed.
P0206: Dashboard sessions and attendance-scanner sessions are tracked separately. Ordinary session replacement affects the corresponding scope. Administrator trusted-browser transfer is an additional security operation: successful replacement of that trust revokes both previous dashboard and scanner scopes. Online pages check session ownership about every five seconds; suspended or offline pages check when they resume or reconnect. The thirty-day phone-pairing period concerns the camera connection and is separate from administrator browser trust and operator authentication.
P0207: The activity-log implementation covered operational record changes and was extended to security and access events: rejected credential attempts, rate-limit rejections, member and scanner sign-ins and explicit sign-out requests, successful password updates, password-reset requests, and report-export requests. Administrators could filter and review these events; passwords, reset tokens, and report contents were excluded. Export logging represented a request rather than verified file-download completion, and closing a browser was not treated as a reliable explicit sign-out. The November 10, 2026 source changes required their database migration and authentication-function deployment before the expanded coverage could be claimed for production. Reset-request capture also depended on Supabase database Auth audit storage being enabled.
P0208: Member account access also included browser registration and approval. The system stored one registered browser per member account and required a new browser to obtain approval from the currently signed-in registered browser when that browser was available. Browser public keys, session challenges, and approval requests were maintained in separate private tables. These records were distinct from member profiles and phone-camera pairings. The migration and required functions were recorded as deployed; controlled member browser-acceptance testing remained pending.
P0209: The system was limited to Iron Grind Fitness Gym. It did not connect to other gyms, external stores, online payment gateways, banks, or mobile wallet services. It did not provide product reservations, AI-based workout or supplement recommendations, medical advice, or automated fitness coaching. Membership progress referred to the time remaining in a membership period and did not measure workout performance or physical changes. Traffic capacity and a maximum number of simultaneous users were not established by the available functional checks. A separate controlled load test and documented physical multi-phone validation remained necessary before making such performance claims.

P0217: Definition of Terms
P0218: Administrator. An authorized user responsible for reviewing applications and managing member, attendance, inventory, sales, and report records.
P0219: Authentication. Verification of an account through Supabase Auth and the applicable administrator device-security checks before protected IronGrind access is authorized.
P0220: Authorization. The determination of which records and operations an authenticated user may access according to the permissions assigned in the system.
P0221: Authorized Staff. A user assigned a staff account to record attendance, register walk-in members, process direct membership renewals and plan changes, approve or reject online registration and renewal requests, and record in-person product sales within the access allowed by the system.
P0222: Centralized Database. The shared data store that organizes linked membership, attendance, inventory, and transaction records for IronGrind.
P0223: Digital Recordkeeping. The electronic storage and retrieval of gym information in place of relying only on paper forms, logbooks, and separate files.
P0224: Gym Management Application. A software application that supports the administration of gym services, such as membership records, attendance monitoring, inventory, and sales.
P0225: Gym Member. An individual with a member record in IronGrind. A linked sign-in account is separate and is available where the system has linked one.
P0226: Health Declaration. Health-related information provided by an applicant as part of membership registration; it is recorded for the gym's stated purposes and does not constitute a medical diagnosis.
P0227: Incomplete Attendance. A visit without a check-out whose check-in date is earlier than the current Philippine calendar date.
P0228: Information Quality. The accuracy, completeness, relevance, and clarity of information presented by a system, considered in this study as a concept for reviewing operational records.
P0229: Input Validation. Checks applied to submitted information, such as required fields, permitted formats, password requirements, and duplicate account identifiers.
P0230: Inventory. The recorded quantities of supplements and merchandise available at the gym, monitored using units appropriate to each product.
P0231: Membership Application. An online request from a prospective member to join the gym. A pending application awaits review by authorized staff or the administrator; a reviewed application retains its decision status.
P0232: Membership History. A record of membership events, including renewals, plan changes, and expired membership periods.
P0233: Membership Progress. A dashboard display showing the time remaining in a member’s recorded membership period.
P0234: Membership Renewal Request. A request submitted by an existing member to renew their membership. It may be submitted at any time and takes effect only after approval by authorized staff or the administrator. An active membership carries forward unused time, while an expired membership restarts on the approval date.
P0235: Membership Subscription. A recorded gym membership plan covering a defined period, with a start date and an expiration date.
P0236: Pending Pairing QR. An unexpired administrator-issued QR that reserves one phone-pairing slot until pairing is completed, ended, or expires.
P0237: Personal Information. Details recorded about an applicant, member, or operator, such as a name, contact information, and account information.
P0238: Phone Pairing. The administrator-authorized connection of a phone browser installation to attendance scanning for the recorded pairing period.
P0239: Progressive Web App (PWA). The installable browser-based attendance scanner that can be launched from a device home screen. Installation does not replace operator authentication, phone pairing, or the internet connection required to save attendance.
P0240: Point of Sale. The stage of an in-person purchase at which the gym records the products, quantities, and transaction amount; IronGrind records the transaction without providing a receipt.
P0241: PostgreSQL. The relational database technology underlying the Supabase database used to store IronGrind records.
P0242: QR Code. A two-dimensional machine-readable code assigned to a member and presented for authorized attendance scanning.
P0243: QR Code-Based Attendance Monitoring. The recording of a member check-in or check-out when an authorized administrator or staff member scans the member QR code.
P0244: Reporting Feature. A feature that organizes membership, payment, attendance, inventory, or sales information into reports available to authorized users according to their system role.
P0245: Role-Based Access Control. The assignment of system permissions according to administrator, staff, and member roles.
P0246: Row Level Security (RLS). Database rules used in Supabase to control which rows a user may read or modify.
P0247: Sales Transaction. A recorded in-person purchase containing the customer details, items or membership charges, quantities, and amount, as applicable.
P0248: Sensitive Personal Information. Under Republic Act No. 10173, information about a person's health is sensitive personal information. In IronGrind, this term refers to health-related declarations collected during registration and handled with restricted access.
P0249: Supabase. The hosted platform IronGrind uses for Auth and its PostgreSQL database. Configured Row Level Security policies and application role checks restrict permitted data access and operations.
P0250: System Quality. The reliability and usability of system functions, discussed as a concept in reviewing how IronGrind supports gym operations.
P0251: User Satisfaction. A user's assessment of their experience with the system, discussed as a concept rather than a measured result of this study.
P0252: User Session. The authenticated access period associated with a particular account sign-in and its permitted dashboard or attendance-scanner scope.
P0253: Trusted Browser. A browser profile and site origin whose public signing key is registered for an administrator account for the configured thirty-day trust period. Its private key remains in browser storage; this does not identify physical hardware.
P0254: Time-Based One-Time Password (TOTP). A short-lived code generated by an enrolled authenticator, used for administrator browser registration, replacement and the implemented recovery verification path.
P0255: Device Approval. An in-website decision by an eligible active trusted administrator session on another browser login request. Approval must be followed by fresh TOTP and successful device proof before trust is transferred.
P0256: Walk-in Customer. An individual who purchases gym products without using a registered member account.
P0257: Web-Based System. An application accessed through a web browser, allowing authorized users to use IronGrind through supported devices.

P0260: REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES
P0261: This chapter reviews literature, applications, and studies relevant to gym membership, attendance monitoring, inventory management, and daily operations. Related Literature discusses technical concepts and frameworks that inform the project. P0262: Related Literature
P0263: Supabase is a hosted platform whose projects include a dedicated PostgreSQL database, Auth and user management, Edge Functions, Realtime, and Storage (Supabase, n.d.-c). These services are relevant to IronGrind because its member profiles, applications, attendance, inventory, and sales records need a centralized data store and controlled access. IronGrind uses Supabase Auth to identify users and database authorization to restrict operations according to administrator, staff, and member roles. The platform is discussed here as supporting technology rather than as a competing gym management application.
P0264: A Quick Response (QR) Code is a standardized two-dimensional barcode symbology for encoding data (International Organization for Standardization, 2024). In IronGrind, a member presents a QR code so that an authorized administrator or staff member can record a check-in or check-out. The code supports member identification within the attendance workflow and replaces handwritten entry with a digital record. It does not perform biometric verification or operate an automated entrance gate. Supported phone cameras provide an additional scanning option under an authorized operator account.
P0265: The DeLone and McLean Information Systems Success Model examines information systems through dimensions such as system quality, information quality, service quality, use, user satisfaction, and net benefits. Al-Kofahi et al. (2025) reviewed 102 scholarly articles and conference papers indexed in Scopus and published between 2012 and 2021. Their weighted analysis identified information quality and user satisfaction as the strongest predictors among the model's internal variables. This framework is relevant to IronGrind because the system's value depends not only on the existence of its pages and database but also on whether its workflows are usable and provide dependable information for members and staff. The model provides a conceptual lens for examining IronGrind, not a measurement framework for this study.

Attendance history is limited to the member's own visits. The catalog allows products to be viewed, while product-sale entries are handled through the administrator or authorized staff workflow rather than through a member checkout process.

P0525: 1. Authenticate Account
P0526: Supabase Auth verifies credentials. Members sign in with the email address on their account; staff and administrators use their assigned usernames. Member registration collects an email and password, not a separate username, and the member dashboard displays the member's full name. The profile role and active-account state determine administrator, staff, or member access, with protected operations checked by server-side authorization. New member registration uses a visible password-strength indicator and blocks passwords that do not meet the Strong requirements: 8 to 128 characters, uppercase and lowercase letters, at least one digit, and at least one symbol. Confirmation must match the selected password. Auth and the member-creation service enforce the password policy on the server; staff-account creation uses a minimum of 12 characters with the same character requirements. Existing sign-in forms continue to accept account credentials without imposing registration validation on login.
P0527: Administrator device authorization is part of User Login rather than an additional main function. Password authentication establishes an Auth session but does not by itself authorize an administrator dashboard or scanner session. The server checks the active role, application scope, current trusted public key and authorized session mapping. A session may access protected operations only after the applicable device-verification workflow completes. Staff and member login retain their role-specific checks.         
P0528: First registration and return visits. The genuine administrator privately enrolls a TOTP authenticator when required and verifies a fresh code to register the first trusted browser. The browser creates an ECDSA P-256 key pair and retains a non-extractable private CryptoKey in IndexedDB; the server stores the public JWK. Trust lasts thirty days from registration. A later password login in the same browser and origin proves possession of that key automatically, without requesting a code on every login. Routine reuse does not extend the trust period. This is browser-profile trust, not a hardware identity: a different browser, origin, cleared storage or unavailable key can require registration again.         
P0529: Replacement while another trusted session is active. A different browser submits a session-bound approval request after password verification. The currently active trusted browser receives an in-website notification and may approve or deny it. The requesting browser must then complete fresh TOTP verification and sign its server-issued challenge. Approval alone does not authorize access. A successful server transaction replaces the trusted key and revokes the previous dashboard and attendance scopes; cancellation, denial or failed verification preserves the previous trust. Requests expire after two minutes, and signing challenges after ten minutes.         
P0530: Availability, logout and recovery. The application polls for approval notifications while the trusted page is active; a heartbeat within two minutes determines whether an eligible approving session is recently active. This is not SMS, operating-system push, or proof that a physical device is online. When no eligible approving session is available, fresh authenticator verification provides the implemented replacement path. Normal logout retains browser trust, while Log out and forget this device revokes it. Lost authenticator access requires verified project-owner recovery. There is no password-only bypass or custom backup-code recovery. Device-signature authorization does not itself change the Auth JWT assurance level to AAL2.         
P0531: Implementation and evidence. admin-device-trust.js coordinates browser-key proof, admin-device-approval.js handles approval notifications, and admin-mfa.js performs enrollment and fresh authenticator verification. The admin-device-trust Edge Function validates the signed-in account and session, verifies the signature and fresh TOTP evidence, and calls service-only completion functions. Database policies and session guards enforce the result independently of visible dialogs. User-supplied CLI output confirms application of migrations through 20261110000004 and deployment of admin-device-trust. These outputs do not establish successful physical two-browser transfer, authenticator use or matching Vercel frontend deployment; those remain acceptance checks.         
P0532: Usernames and email addresses are checked for duplicates without regard to capitalization or surrounding spaces. Registration shows availability feedback and rejects identifiers already in use; server checks support this validation. Dashboard and attendance-scanner sessions are tracked as separate scopes. Ordinary replacement affects its own scope, while successful administrator trusted-browser transfer explicitly revokes both old scopes. Trust transfer and explicit forget-device revocation are additional administrator security controls, not ordinary scope-only replacement.

P1844: Forms
P1845: The system uses registration, profile, renewal, member, inventory, sales, staff-account, and phone-pairing forms. Members submit online registration or renewal information and maintain their permitted profile details. Administrators review online requests, maintain products and stock, and manage staff and pairing access. Authorized staff use counter forms for walk-in registration, direct renewal, active-member plan change, and product purchases, and use Requests approval for online registration and renewal decisions. Attendance uses camera scanning under an authorized operator account. Completed payments and purchases are recorded as transactions; receipts are not provided to customers. The form also checks username and Gmail availability, phone format, password strength, and matching confirmation. Staff directory access and activation controls remain administrator-only. The Requests approval view separately presents pending online registration and renewal summaries for authorized staff and administrator decisions, with in-person payment confirmation before approval.

P2033: Figure 63 presents the original ten-week development schedule for IronGrind, from August 24 to October 28, 2026. The activity and date columns identify the work assigned to each scheduled week. As clarified by the proponents, Planning occupies Weeks 1-4 and consists of identifying a potential client, conducting the first client interview to identify problems and needs, project conceptualization, and drafting the proposal. Design occupies Weeks 5-8 and covers interface design, prototype development, client feedback, and refinement. Development occupies Weeks 9-10 and covers frontend, backend, database, API, and supporting work alongside the project chapters. The four planning labels reflect the activities reported by the proponents. The existing date ranges are retained as schedule assignments and do not independently establish the actual completion dates of those activities.
P2034: Each colored bar locates an activity within its assigned planned week. The chart does not record actual completion, measured effort, evaluation outcomes, or production readiness. Its three phase groups organize the original schedule, while the Agile methodology describes how development and refinement can repeat within and beyond that plan. Later repository revisions and deployment evidence must be reported separately rather than retroactively treated as work completed within the original ten weeks.
