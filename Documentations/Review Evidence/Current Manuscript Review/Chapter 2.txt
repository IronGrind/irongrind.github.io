P0001: Chapter 2
P0002: REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES
P0003: This chapter reviews literature, applications, and studies relevant to gym membership, attendance monitoring, inventory management, and daily operations. Related Literature discusses technical concepts and frameworks that inform the project. Related Applications examines existing commercial gym management platforms, while Related Studies presents research on comparable systems. Together, these sources provide a basis for IronGrind: A Fitness Gym Membership and Operations Management System for Iron Grind Fitness Gym in Purok Mangga, Sulangon, Dapitan City, Zamboanga del Norte.
P0004: Related Literature
P0005: Supabase is a hosted platform whose projects include a dedicated PostgreSQL database, Auth and user management, Edge Functions, Realtime, and Storage (Supabase, n.d.-c). These services are relevant to IronGrind because its member profiles, applications, attendance, inventory, and sales records need a centralized data store and controlled access. IronGrind uses Supabase Auth to identify users and database authorization to restrict operations according to administrator, staff, and member roles. The platform is discussed here as supporting technology rather than as a competing gym management application.
P0006: A Quick Response (QR) Code is a standardized two-dimensional barcode symbology for encoding data (International Organization for Standardization, 2024). In IronGrind, a member presents a QR code so that an authorized administrator or staff member can record a check-in or check-out. The code supports member identification within the attendance workflow and replaces handwritten entry with a digital record. It does not perform biometric verification or operate an automated entrance gate. Supported phone cameras provide an additional scanning option under an authorized operator account.
P0007: The DeLone and McLean Information Systems Success Model examines information systems through dimensions such as system quality, information quality, service quality, use, user satisfaction, and net benefits. Al-Kofahi et al. (2025) reviewed 102 scholarly articles and conference papers indexed in Scopus and published between 2012 and 2021. Their weighted analysis identified information quality and user satisfaction as the strongest predictors among the model's internal variables. This framework is relevant to IronGrind because the system's value depends not only on the existence of its pages and database but also on whether its workflows are usable and provide dependable information for members and staff. The model provides a conceptual lens for examining IronGrind, not a measurement framework for this study.
P0008: The National Privacy Commission was constituted on March 7, 2016, under the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 (National Privacy Commission, 2026). Section 11 of the Act states the principles of transparency, legitimate purpose, and proportionality; Section 3(l)(2) includes health information among sensitive personal information (Republic Act No. 10173, 2012). These provisions are relevant to IronGrind because the system stores account, contact, emergency contact, and health-related information.
P0009: Supabase (n.d.-b) describes Row Level Security as a database mechanism for restricting access to rows through policies.
P0010: Supabase (n.d.-d) explains that an Auth access token includes a session identifier associated with an Auth session record.
P0011: Supabase (n.d.-a) recommends reviewing query indexes and performing load testing, preferably in a staging environment, before production use.
P0012: Related Applications
P0013: Gymdesk presents a commercial approach to centralized gym administration. Its founder account describes the development of the platform from a side project into a service used by gyms (Gymdesk, 2025). Its relevance to IronGrind lies in bringing membership and operational information into an organized system. IronGrind applies that principle to a single gym through linked member, attendance, inventory, and transaction records. Features offered by a commercial platform are points of comparison; they do not establish which functions IronGrind implements or demonstrate the performance of the local system.
P0014: GymMaster advertises member management, billing, point-of-sale, inventory, access control, and online sign-ups (GymMaster, n.d.). GYMMANAGER advertises customer management, sales, access control, facility operations, and analytics (GYMMANAGER, n.d.). These vendor-described functions provide comparison points for the commercial fitness-management sector; they are not independent evaluations of either product. IronGrind is designed around the workflows identified for one local gym and does not claim to implement every function advertised by these platforms, such as payment integrations, access-control hardware, or dedicated mobile applications.
P0015: Related Studies
P0016: Villanueva, Sevilla, and Verecio (2025) examined manual attendance, subscription monitoring, sales recording, and reporting at Olympic Fitness Gym in Tacloban City. Their study proposed a management system integrating QR code technology to address limitations in manual records and monitoring. It is relevant to IronGrind because both projects concern organizing membership and attendance information in a gym setting. IronGrind uses operator-scanned member QR codes and stores attendance separately from current membership details. The comparison supports the choice of related functions; it does not establish measured improvements for IronGrind.
P0017: Bulaclac, Pastorfide, Herrera, and Tambio (2025) developed a QR-enabled cashless payment and product-monitoring system for a Philippine fitness enterprise. They reported an 80% reduction in average transaction time, 95% accuracy in financial and inventory tracking, and a mean System Usability Scale score of 88 out of 100. These are findings for their evaluated system. The study is relevant to IronGrind because it examines linked operational records and QR-supported gym services. IronGrind records payments made in person and does not implement the cashless payment process studied by these authors. Its own accuracy, efficiency, and usability must be evaluated separately.
P0018: Babili (2026) presents GymFlow in an author-posted project report on an integrated fitness-center management system. The report describes membership administration, QR attendance, workout planning, payment recording, and progress analytics. It reports QR accuracy of 98.7%, processing time of 1.2 seconds, support for 200 concurrent users, and a System Usability Scale score of 85.6. These figures are author-reported results for GymFlow, not independently verified findings or results for IronGrind. The report provides a comparison for combining membership and attendance functions, while its workout planning, analytics, and simulated payment gateway differ from the scope of IronGrind. The author-posted report should be distinguished from a peer-reviewed journal article.
P0019: Synthesis
P0020: The related literature identifies concepts that inform the design of IronGrind. A centralized database links operational records, authentication identifies users, and authorization limits permitted activities. QR codes support the attendance workflow, while the Information Systems Success Model provides a conceptual perspective on dependable information and usability. The privacy literature also highlights the need for careful handling of personal and health-related records. These concepts guide system design; they do not establish that the system has achieved measured improvements or verified legal compliance.
P0021: The related applications illustrate how commercial platforms organize gym services. Gymdesk, GymMaster, and GYMMANAGER combine membership administration with other operational functions. Their relevance lies in demonstrating existing approaches to centralized gym management. Their broader payment, access-control, and mobile capabilities are not automatically requirements of IronGrind, which is designed for the identified workflows of a single gym.
P0022: The related studies describe comparable efforts to organize gym operations. Villanueva et al. (2025) discuss attendance and subscription monitoring, Bulaclac et al. (2025) examine payment and product monitoring, and Babili (2026) presents an integrated QR-enabled gym system. These studies provide points of comparison for membership, attendance, inventory, and sales functions. Their reported results belong to their respective systems and are not evaluation results for IronGrind.
P0023: Taken together, the reviewed literature, applications, and studies support the development of a system tailored to Iron Grind Fitness Gym. IronGrind combines membership records, online application and renewal review, authorized staff operations, QR-based attendance, inventory using product-appropriate units, in-person transaction recording, and administrative reports. Payments are handled at the gym, and the system records transactions without providing receipts. The project applies these established concepts to local operational requirements; any claims of improved accuracy, efficiency, or usability require evaluation of IronGrind itself.
P0024: The additional technical literature supports explicit input validation, active-role authorization, session management, and a clear distinction between verification and performance evaluation. In the system design, online request review remains available to authorized staff and administrators, phone-pairing reservations share the configured capacity, and attendance states reflect whether a check-out was recorded. These are implemented workflow rules whose correctness must be checked against the source code and database. The literature provides context for these choices without establishing that IronGrind has completed a load test or achieved measured gains in efficiency or usability.
P0025: These concepts also informed the separation of operational records from supporting security records. In IronGrind, member browser registration, session challenges, and approval requests were maintained in private tables, while membership, attendance, inventory, and sales records remained in the public application schema. The member browser-approval workflow extended account authorization without introducing another main system function. This design application did not establish measured improvements in security or usability; controlled browser-acceptance testing remained necessary.
