1 [style=;bold=2] REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES
2 [style=;bold=0] 
3 [style=;bold=2]           This chapter reviews literature, applications, and studies relevant to gym membership, attendance monitoring, inventory management, and daily operations. Related Literature discusses technical concepts and frameworks that inform the project. Related Applications examines existing commercial gym management platforms, while Related Studies presents research on comparable systems. Together, these sources provide a basis for IronGrind: A Fitness Gym Membership and Operations Management System for Iron Grind Fitness Gym in Purok Mangga, Sulangon, Dapitan City, Zamboanga del Norte.
4 [style=Heading2;bold=1] Related Literature
5 [style=;bold=0]           Supabase is an open-source backend platform built on PostgreSQL. Y Combinator (n.d.) describes its database, authentication, Row Level Security, and real-time data capabilities. These concepts are relevant to IronGrind because its member profiles, applications, attendance, inventory, and sales records need a centralized data store and controlled access. IronGrind uses Supabase Auth to identify users and database authorization to restrict operations according to administrator, staff, and member roles. The platform is discussed here as supporting technology rather than as a competing gym management application.
6 [style=;bold=0]           QR codes provide a machine-readable means of representing information that a camera or scanner can read. In IronGrind, a member presents a QR code so that an authorized administrator or staff member can record a check-in or check-out. The code supports member identification within the attendance workflow and replaces handwritten entry with a digital record. It does not perform biometric verification or operate an automated entrance gate. Supported phone cameras provide an additional scanning option under an authorized operator account.
7 [style=;bold=0]         The DeLone and McLean Information Systems Success Model remains a widely used framework for examining information systems through dimensions such as system quality, information quality, service quality, use, user satisfaction, and net benefits. A recent literature review by Al-Kofahi, Hassan, and Mohamad (2025), which analyzed 102 studies published between 2012 and 2021, confirms that the model is still commonly applied and that information quality and user satisfaction are among its strongest predictors of system success. This framework is relevant to IronGrind because the system’s value depends not only on the existence of its pages and database but also on whether its workflows are usable and provide dependable information for members and staff. The model provides a conceptual lens for examining IronGrind, not a measurement framework for this study.
8 [style=;bold=0]        The National Privacy Commission was constituted in March 2016 to oversee the implementation of the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 (National Privacy Commission, 2026). The Act establishes principles for processing personal information, including transparency, legitimate purpose, and proportionality, and identifies health information as sensitive personal information (Republic Act No. 10173, 2012). This regulatory framework is relevant to IronGrind because the system stores account, contact, emergency contact, and health-related information. IronGrind uses Supabase Auth for identity and session management, profile roles to distinguish administrators and members, and database authorization for permitted operations. Privileged staff-created account operations are routed through a server-side Edge Function that checks for an active administrator or staff role and restricts staff to permitted registration operations. The gym should explain the purposes for collecting information, limit collection and access to legitimate operational needs, protect the records, and establish appropriate retention and handling procedures.
9 [style=Heading2;bold=1] Related Applications
10 [style=;bold=0]          Gymdesk was founded in 2016 as “Martial Arts on Rails,” built by Eran Galperin, a Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu black belt and software developer who was working nights and weekends while holding a full-time technology position (Gymdesk, 2025). The platform was built on a simple philosophy: create software that is actually easy to use, offer transparent pricing, and solve real business problems for gym owners (Gymdesk, 2025). By 2026, Gymdesk served thousands of gyms worldwide, growing from a solo founder operation to a company with 16 employees and $3 million in annual recurring revenue (Gymdesk, 2026). This system is relevant to IronGrind because it demonstrated that the centralized-database approach was both practical and scalable. Gymdesk’s member accounts bring together memberships, attendance history, billing, documents, and notes in one place so that staff can see a complete picture of each person’s activity and status (Gymdesk, n.d.). IronGrind adopts this centralization logic through its Supabase PostgreSQL database, which links profiles, members, applications, membership history, attendance, products, sales, and sale items. However, IronGrind rejects the commercial scale of Gymdesk. Iron Grind is a single-location gym with in-person payments. It needed the centralization idea, not the commercial ecosystem.
11 [style=;bold=0]            GymMaster describes features for managing member profiles, memberships, billing, access control, point-of-sale, inventory, and online sign-ups (GymMaster, n.d.). GYMMANAGER likewise presents a system for customer management, sales, access control, facility operations, and analytics (GYMMANAGER, n.d.). These commercial systems are relevant to IronGrind because they illustrate the range of functions available in the fitness-management sector. Unlike these broad commercial platforms, IronGrind is designed around the specific workflows and scale identified for one local gym. The reviewed commercial platforms offer broader capabilities than IronGrind, such as payment integrations, access-control hardware, and dedicated mobile applications. Vendor descriptions show that these functions exist in the market, but they do not establish that IronGrind includes them or that they are required by the local gym.
12 [style=Heading2;bold=1] Related Studies
13 [style=;bold=0]          Villanueva, Sevilla, and Verecio (2025) examined the Olympic Fitness Gym in Tacloban City and found the same conditions that would later exist at Iron Grind: manual processes for recording sales, generating reports, and monitoring attendance and subscriptions, with records scattered across paper forms and logbooks. Their proposed QR-enabled system was designed to replace these manual processes, not to upgrade an existing digital system. This study is relevant to IronGrind because both gyms needed a first system, not an upgrade. The parallel is direct: Iron Grind opened in July 2026 with no digital system at all, only paper forms and handwritten logbooks. The Villanueva study confirms that QR technology offers a practical, low-cost solution for Philippine gyms. IronGrind adopts the same staff-operated QR workflow, though its attendance records are stored separately from membership records to preserve distinct operational histories.
14 [style=;bold=0]           Bulaclac, Pastorfide, Herrera, and Tambio (2025) developed a QR-enabled cashless payment and product-monitoring system for a Philippine fitness enterprise. Their study reported measurable improvements after implementation: an 80% reduction in average transaction time, a 95% accuracy rate in financial and inventory tracking, and a mean System Usability Scale score of 88/100. This study is relevant to IronGrind because it validated that integrating membership, inventory, and sales modules produces measurable gains. IronGrind adapts this insight to a cash-based context. It does not process cashless payments; Iron Grind operates on in-person transactions. But the integration logic linking membership, inventory, and sales in one platform—is the same. The Bulaclac study demonstrates that integrating operational modules produces measurable gains, and IronGrind applies that principle to a gym with in-person payments.
15 [style=;bold=0]         Babili (2026) developed GymFlow, a smart gym management system at Arab International University in Syria that directly addresses the same problems IronGrind solves: manual recordkeeping, inefficient attendance tracking, and fragmented membership data. GymFlow integrates membership management, QR code-based attendance tracking, personalized workout planning, payment processing, and progress analytics into a unified web-based platform. It was evaluated with measurable results: a 98.7% QR attendance accuracy rate, a 1.2-second QR processing time, support for 200 concurrent users with sub-500 ms response times, and a System Usability Scale score of 85.6 (Babili, 2026). This system is particularly relevant to IronGrind because its stated limitations mirror IronGrind’s deliberate scope decisions. The GymFlow documentation explicitly notes that the system has no formal security audit, no native mobile app, and uses a simulated payment gateway rather than processing real payments (Babili, 2026). These are the exact boundaries set in Chapter 1 for IronGrind. GymFlow demonstrates that a scoped, web-based, QR-enabled gym management system is a recognized and evaluated approach in academic contexts, not an incomplete version of a commercial platform.
16 [style=Heading2;bold=1] Synthesis
17 [style=;bold=0]             The related literature identifies concepts that inform the design of IronGrind. A centralized database links operational records, authentication identifies users, and authorization limits permitted activities. QR codes support the attendance workflow, while the Information Systems Success Model provides a conceptual perspective on dependable information and usability. The privacy literature also highlights the need for careful handling of personal and health-related records. These concepts guide system design; they do not establish that the system has achieved measured improvements or verified legal compliance.
18 [style=;bold=0]               The related applications illustrate how commercial platforms organize gym services. Gymdesk, GymMaster, and GYMMANAGER combine membership administration with other operational functions. Their relevance lies in demonstrating existing approaches to centralized gym management. Their broader payment, access-control, and mobile capabilities are not automatically requirements of IronGrind, which is designed for the identified workflows of a single gym.
19 [style=;bold=0]            The related studies describe comparable efforts to organize gym operations. Villanueva et al. (2025) discuss attendance and subscription monitoring, Bulaclac et al. (2025) examine payment and product monitoring, and Babili (2026) presents an integrated QR-enabled gym system. These studies provide points of comparison for membership, attendance, inventory, and sales functions. Their reported results belong to their respective systems and are not evaluation results for IronGrind.
20 [style=;bold=2]              Taken together, the reviewed literature, applications, and studies support the development of a system tailored to Iron Grind Fitness Gym. IronGrind combines membership records, online application and renewal review, authorized staff operations, QR-based attendance, inventory using product-appropriate units, in-person transaction recording, and administrative reports. Payments are handled at the gym, and the system records transactions without providing receipts. The project applies these established concepts to local operational requirements; any claims of improved accuracy, efficiency, or usability require evaluation of IronGrind itself.
